## Moving DOGE out: network choice and self-custody

Before you withdraw anything, confirm which network your receiving wallet supports. DOGE moves on its own proof-of-work chain, and it also exists as a token on BNB Smart Chain (BEP-20). Binance.US supports both for DOGE withdrawals. Pick a network your destination wallet doesn't handle and the coins can be lost, which is the single most expensive mistake in this whole process. DOGE deposits don't require a memo, so there's no memo field to get wrong, but the network selector still matters.

On storage, the split is custodial versus non-custodial. Leaving DOGE on an exchange means the exchange holds the keys and you trust its reserves and its withdrawal processing. A wallet you control, whether software on your phone or a hardware device kept offline, means you hold a recovery phrase and are the only backup for it. Wallets like Ledger, Exodus, and Trust Wallet are examples of the self-custody type. Back up the phrase on paper, not in a screenshot.

There's a middle path worth knowing: keep a small working balance on the exchange for buying and selling, and move anything you plan to hold for months to a wallet you control. 👉 [Create your OKX account before you decide where to store the coins](https://okx.com/join/CASH20)

## Three things about DOGE itself worth knowing before you buy

**Supply never stops.** DOGE has no maximum supply. New coins are issued at roughly 10,000 per minute, and the inflation rate declines as the total supply grows because the same absolute number is divided into a larger base. The creators made it inflationary on purpose to encourage spending rather than hoarding. For a holder, that means price gains depend on demand growing faster than a fixed schedule of new coins.

**The drawdown is the context.** The all-time high was $0.7376 on May 8, 2021. At around $0.059 today that's a 92% decline. Market cap sits near $9.23B against a total supply of about 156.78 billion DOGE, with 24-hour volume around $549.59M. Volatility in both directions is the normal state of this asset, not an anomaly.

**Taxes follow the disposal, not the purchase.** The IRS treats DOGE as property. Selling it, swapping it for another coin, or spending it can create a taxable gain or loss. Buying and holding generally doesn't. Keep the records anyway, because the reporting burden lands on the sale, not the buy.

**Why can't I use a credit card for DOGE on Coinbase?** Coinbase doesn't accept credit cards for DOGE purchases. Debit cards, bank accounts, and wire transfers do work, and PayPal is available to US customers for buying.

**What does OKX's 1:1 reserve claim mean?** OKX states that customer funds are backed one-to-one and links to a proof-of-reserves page so you can verify the claim against published balances rather than taking it on faith.

**Can I stake DOGE?** No. DOGE runs on proof-of-work and is merge-mined with Litecoin, so there's no staking mechanism. Any product advertising DOGE staking yield is doing something other than securing the Dogecoin network.

**Do I need to verify my identity?** In the US, yes. KYC/AML verification is a legal requirement on regulated platforms, and trading stays locked until it clears.
